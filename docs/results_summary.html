A reciprocal phage shift tracks HPV status

A phage differential-abundance analysis across 2,921 vOTUs. Two confounds built into the cohort are controlled explicitly rather than set aside. What survives is a reciprocal shift — Lactobacillus phages down, Atopobium vaginae phages up — unanimous in both directions, among the phages whose hosts can be identified.

1. Most “markers” are a design artefact, not biology

Of 596 testable vOTUs, 575 pass the depth-controlled group test. That is 96% — implausible for real differential abundance, and the presence pattern explains why. The two arms were generated from disjoint ViroForge collections (24 for cases, 16 for controls), so a phage in one collection and not the other separates the groups perfectly, with no biology involved.

2. The informative subset: 74 shared-arm markers

Restricting to phages detected in at least five samples of both arms leaves 95 vOTUs, of which 74 clear the depth-controlled threshold. Effect sizes here are moderate (median |δ| ≈ 0.54), and the direction is consistent: 49 depleted in HPV+ against 25 enriched.

3. The Lactobacillus signal

29 of the 74 markers are phages of Lactobacillus species. All 29 are depleted in HPV+ — no exceptions — with a median log2 fold change of −1.16. This holds specifically among markers whose host could be assigned: of the 41 host-assigned markers 33 are depleted, against 16 of 33 unassigned (see Limitations). Host assignments come from CHERRY and are independently corroborated by iPHoP, whose own top predicted hosts across the cohort are L. mulieris (163), L. jensenii (159), and Lacticaseibacillus species.

4. The shift is reciprocal, not one-sided

Splitting the host-assigned markers by bacterial genus gives a cleaner result than Lactobacillus alone. The two vaginal genera present point in opposite directions, with no exceptions in either: all 29 Lactobacillus phages are depleted in HPV+, and all 7 Atopobium vaginae phages are enriched. 36 of 36 markers go the biologically expected way.

5. The two confounds, and how they were handled

Confound 1 — sequencing depth is coupled to severity

Coverage was scaled with antler size during generation (5× at 8 mm → 20× at 63 mm) while all controls received a flat 10×. HPV+ therefore carries ~1.5× the depth of HPV− by construction, and detection rises with depth.

Handled: every vOTU was tested three ways — raw, rarefied to common coverage, and with log(coverage) partialled out. A marker counts as robust only if it clears both the rarefied and depth-adjusted tests; 14 that passed only the raw test are labelled depth_suspect. In the severity analysis, 418 vOTUs tracked antler size and 337 survived coverage adjustment.

Confound 2 — the arms came from disjoint genome collections

Cases were drawn from ViroForge collection 24, controls from collection 16. Phages unique to one collection separate the groups perfectly regardless of biology.

Handled: every marker carries a presence_pattern label. The 421 exclusive markers are reported separately as a demonstration that the pipeline detects presence/absence differences; only the 74 shared-arm markers are treated as informative about abundance.

7. Limitations

  • Statistical control cannot undo a design confound. Rarefaction and covariate adjustment reduce depth-driven inflation; they do not erase the fact that coverage and severity were coupled at generation. Residual confounding cannot be excluded.
  • Host prediction is incomplete, and the gap is not random. Only 1,175 of 2,921 vOTUs received an iPHoP host call; within the 74 markers, 41 have a host and 33 do not. Host prediction works by matching against known bacteria, so it succeeds for well-studied hosts like Lactobacillus and fails for obscure ones. Those two groups behave differently: 33 of 41 host-assigned markers are depleted in HPV+ (median log2FC −1.03), against 16 of 33 unassigned (median +0.60). The depletion signal is therefore concentrated in the identifiable half of the community — among phages we cannot name, there is no clear direction. The defensible claim is about identifiable Lactobacillus phages, not about the phage community as a whole.
  • Lifestyle calls look skewed. 71 of 74 markers were called virulent by BACPHLIP, lopsided enough to suspect the tool performs poorly on short contigs rather than reflecting real lifestyle distribution.
  • MMseqs2 nr taxonomy was omitted — the shared index is 2.1 TB on a mount reading at 7–10 MB/s, so one pass would take ~81 days. Taxonomy comes from PhaGCN and vConTACT3; hosts from CHERRY and iPHoP.
  • Synthetic data. Effect sizes are not transferable to real cervicovaginal samples.
